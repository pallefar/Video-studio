<objective>
Measure, decide, and write it down.

The last unchecked box in M10 reads: benchmark Fun-Camera A14B GGUF plus the four-step LoRA for
latency and VRAM on the 3090, and let it decide the 14B-versus-5B default. Phase success criterion 4
adds that the decision must be written down.

Everything needed to do that now exists. Plan 03-02 rebuilt Fun-Camera as a correct two-expert graph
with a camera embedding that is both connected and shape-matched to its latent. Plan 03-03 built the
four-step accelerated arm and a harness that measures latency and samples device VRAM while the run
is in flight, writing a comparison to a file. Plan 03-01 built the dense 5B so there is a second arm
to compare against. Plan 03-07 confirmed the lane and produced the first clips.

Two things about this measurement are worth stating plainly before it runs. The accelerator's
compatibility with quantised checkpoints is unconfirmed — research recorded it as an assumption and
nothing on a CPU box could settle it, so "the fast arm does not exist" is a possible and legitimate
result that must reach the decision as a measured fact rather than as a missing row. And this is the
first time in this project's history that a camera preset can actually affect its output, because the
node carrying the camera motion was unreachable from the graph's output until plan 03-02 connected
it; whatever the benchmark says about speed, someone should look at whether the camera moves.

This is runbook step 8, phase success criterion 4, and milestone M10.6.

Purpose: replace an open decision with a measured one.
Output: a durable comparison record, a decided default applied everywhere it is named, and a ticked
milestone.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:decision" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 2: The 14B-versus-5B default</name>
  <decision>Which model becomes the default for preset generation and storyboard shot generation: the two-expert 14B at full steps, the two-expert 14B with the four-step accelerator, or the dense 5B.</decision>
  <context>
This is the decision roadmap-v2 §9 anticipated when it named the 5B the fallback "if quality or speed
disappoints", and it is M10.6's entire purpose. Task 1's comparison record is in front of you with
every arm's latency, peak device VRAM, step budget and checkpoint filenames, measured on one
instrument under identical conditions. Plan 03-07's clips are available for the quality half, and any
boundary sweep clips with them.

Three things frame the choice beyond the numbers.

The trade is not purely speed. The wan lane is exclusive, so a generation's latency is time the render
lane cannot use the card, and on a single twenty-four gigabyte machine that is the real cost of a slow
default. But this is a batch, overnight-friendly lane by design — v1's rule that wan jobs never block
a render still holds — so latency matters less here than it would on an interactive path.

VRAM headroom has a second consumer. Roadmap Phase 5 trains identity LoRAs on this same exclusive
lane, overnight. A default that leaves the card comfortable is worth something that does not appear in
this benchmark at all.

The accelerated arm may not exist. If Task 1 recorded it as a failure because the four-step LoRA does
not apply to a quantised checkpoint, the choice is between a slow 14B and a fast 5B, and the option to
revisit is switching the 14B to a non-quantised BF16 checkpoint — which the licence register permits,
since the no-FP8 rule forbids FP8 specifically and BF16 is compliant — at a VRAM cost this benchmark
can predict.

Whatever is chosen becomes the default in three places that each name a model by string: the preset
route, the storyboard route, and the MCP generation tool's default argument. The model roster keeps
every arm available regardless; this decides only what a caller gets when they do not choose.
  </context>
