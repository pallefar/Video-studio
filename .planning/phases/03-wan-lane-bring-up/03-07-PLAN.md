<objective>
Generate the first real clips, and prove the lane rule holds when there is something real on the
card.

Milestone M9.1 has been open since the generative lane shipped: a Wan 2.2 quantised model loading
and generating a five-second clip. Everything around it — the queue, the exclusive lock, the
approval gate, the provenance chain — was proven at the time and has been green since. The one
thing never done is the generation.

Do it in the runbook's order, smallest first, because three things change between an image still
and a two-expert video clip, and a failure that could be any of them costs a second session to
diagnose. An image still proves the executor, the injection, the output collection and the asset
path. The dense 5B adds the video output stage. The two-expert 14B adds the wiring plan 03-02
rebuilt and that nothing has yet executed.

Then prove the lane rule under real load. The lock is already proven correct; what has never
happened is a render job arriving while a real model is resident on the card for the other lane.

This is runbook step 7 and phase success criteria 2 and 3.

Purpose: close M9.1 and confirm the exclusivity guarantee with real workloads.
Output: real assets in the library, measured figures per model, and an observed interleave.
</objective>

Confirm both halves of the exclusivity claim with real models on the card, not just the primitive.

First run the existing exclusivity suite on this host against the real Redis the workers use, and
record that it passes here rather than only in the abstract. It proves the primitive; it is the
baseline the live observation is interpreted against.

Then the live interleave. Start a wan-lane generation long enough to work with — the 14B at five
seconds is ideal since Task 1 measured how long it takes — and while it is running, submit a render
job through the panel. Then observe three things and record each with its evidence.

That the render did not run concurrently: the render's own stage should have found the lock held by
the wan lane and re-raised it as transient, which the worker logs. Capture the log line naming the
holder. Confirm from the render job's state that it was retried rather than failed, since a
permanent failure on lock contention would be a real defect in a path nothing has exercised under
load.

That the queue was never blocked: the render job must have been accepted and queued immediately,
with only its execution deferred. Capture its queued timestamp relative to the wan generation's
start. This is the claim that distinguishes a correct two-lane design from a global mutex, and it is
the one a user actually feels.

That both models were never resident: sample device VRAM through the interleave and record the
maximum. Compare it against the render lane's established baseline from Phase 1's smoke run and
against the wan generation's own peak from Task 1. A maximum that exceeds either alone by roughly
the other's footprint would mean both lanes had weights on the card at once, which the lock is
supposed to make impossible. Record the numbers even when they are unremarkable — an unremarkable
number recorded is evidence, and an unremarkable number remembered is not.

Then let the wan generation finish and confirm the render proceeds and completes. The lock's release
path is proven by test, but the full cycle under real load closes the loop.

If anything here fails, do not fix it in this plan. Record the observation precisely — which of the
three claims failed, with the log lines and timestamps — and stop. A defect in the exclusivity path
is a bug against the lock or the stages, not something to be worked around during a hardware
session, and it needs a test that reproduces it before a fix means anything.
