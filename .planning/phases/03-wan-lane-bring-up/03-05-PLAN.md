<objective>
Make the hardware session boring.

Everything before this plan is desk work. Everything after it burns either the workstation's
evenings or a rented card's hourly rate. The difference between those two costs is almost
entirely determined by how much is decided in advance, and right now three things are not.

The model weights have no fetcher for anything but the one proven 2.1 template — the workstation
guidance for everything else is a markdown table a human reads and types. Every filename in that
table is a claim about a template, and plans 03-01 through 03-04 changed most of those templates.
The node packs are cloned at floating HEAD with no commit recorded anywhere, so the node
signatures this phase verified against upstream source have no way to be checked against what is
actually installed — research Pitfall 3 names this precisely, and contrasts it with Phase 1,
which did pin its engine dependency. And the runbook's steps 6 through 8 are prose, while steps
1 through 5 are commands.

Purpose: turn the remaining runbook steps into commands, and make every filename and version the
hardware session depends on a checked fact rather than a typed one.
Output: one shared fetcher driven by the templates themselves, pinned node packs, and docs that
work on a rented box as readily as on the 3090.
</objective>
